Ways to light the hub

Each concept runs a line of addressable RGB light around the hub, following its rounded shape. All four are ray-traced with the same blue-to-orange gradient, in a dark room, in room light, and switched off. None has a vent grid on top: the air hides in a seam.

How brightness was set: in room light, every concept uses the same realistic LED output (about 40% of full), so those images compare fairly. In the dark room, each is dimmed to a comfortable night level, as the software would do. Plastic's optical properties are estimates; a small test print confirms the real look.

Test plate · waiting for your OK to print

Does a thin white skin really glow?

One 25-minute print answers the question behind A, E and F. Each coupon is a step wedge: five windows thinned to 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 1.0 and 1.2 mm, so you can see which thickness looks best rather than testing one guess.

Coupon H · horizontal

71 × 24 × 4 mm tile. Skins face the bed, exactly as the hub's roof would print. Numbers engraved on the back (4 = 0.4 mm … 12 = 1.2 mm).

Coupon V · vertical

71 × 22 mm wall on a 14 mm foot. Pocketed from the back, so the skins are part of a vertical wall, as side lighting would print. Numbers on the foot.

Material

Your white PLA, about 12 g.

Settings in Bambu Studio

Bambu A1 Mini, 0.4 nozzle, your white PLA profile, textured or smooth plate. 0.20 mm layers, so every skin is a whole number of layers. Bottom shell layers: 6 and wall loops: 3, so each skin prints solid rather than as infill. Infill 15%. No supports: V's window tops are 10 mm bridges, which the A1 Mini handles.

How to test

In a dark room, lay each coupon over your phone's flashlight and look from the light side: H's bottom face, V's front face. Note which number gives an even glow without showing a hot spot. Then check both in daylight with the light off, to see whether the thin areas show. The real LEDs sit 5–6 mm back, which spreads the light more than a phone held against it.

Files

home-devices/tests/skin_test_plate.3mf has both coupons placed on the 180 × 180 mm plate. The individual STLs are alongside. When you approve, either open the 3MF in Bambu Studio and press Slice, then Print, or connect your computer to this session and I'll do that part for you.

Concept E · Round 2 · 52 LEDs · one strip

Glow hem

The shell floats 5 mm off the table, like D. Its lowest 8 mm of wall is thinned from the inside to a 0.8 mm skin. One strip behind it lights the band and spills out underneath onto the table.

Diffuser

The shell's own white PLA, thinned to 0.8 mm. No new filament.

Air

Underneath, through the gap around the small base.

LEDs

One strip does both the band and the underglow.

Good

  • Simplest light path: one strip, no extra diffuser part.
  • A bold, wide band that reads from every side.

Watch

  • The band is wide, so it's more lamp than line.
  • Colours pale where they pass through white plastic.
Concept F · Round 2 · 104 LEDs · two strips

Reveal line + underglow

C's floating-lid line, made without translucent filament: a shallow 0.8 mm groove runs round the sides near the top, and the groove's floor is a thin white skin with a strip behind it. Below, D's underglow. A black channel boxes in each strip, so light only leaves through the line and the bottom edge.

Diffuser

White PLA skin at the bottom of a groove. No new filament.

Air

Underneath, plus the gap at the base.

LEDs

Two strips: one for the line (status), one for the underglow (ambient). Each can be controlled on its own.

Good

  • The floating-lid look in one material, as one shell.
  • When off, the groove reads as a crisp shadow line, like C.
  • Line for status, underglow for mood: two separate channels.

Watch

  • Depends on the vertical-skin result from coupon V.
  • Two strips on one data line: more wiring, and the brightness cap matters more.

Round 1

The first four concepts

Concept A · 48 LEDs · 289 mm path

Hidden halo

The roof is thinned from the inside to a 0.6 mm skin along a band 7 mm in from the edge. Off, the top looks like plain white plastic. On, a soft line of light appears through it.

Diffuser

The shell itself: 0.6 mm of your white PLA. Nothing new to buy.

Air

In through a 1 mm gap at the shadow line under the shell; out through the ports window.

Parts

Shell, base, a black light channel. Same count as now.

Good

  • The most seamless. When off, there's no visible light feature at all.
  • No translucent filament needed.

Watch

  • Softest and least vivid; colours pale slightly through white plastic.
  • Depends on printing a consistent 0.6 mm skin. A 15-minute test coupon settles it.
  • A faint line may show when off, where the roof is thin. Check the "off" render.
Concept B · 48 LEDs · 293 mm path

Light seam

A 4.6 mm slot follows the top's shape. A 3 mm frosted insert sits in it, slightly below the surface, leaving hairline gaps on both sides. The gaps are the vents.

Diffuser

A separate insert in translucent white filament (e.g. Bambu PLA Translucent or translucent PETG). One small print.

Air

Through the 0.8 mm gaps either side of the insert, across the whole perimeter.

Parts

Shell, base, light channel, frosted insert.

Good

  • Crisp and vivid: the line reads clearly, and colours stay saturated.
  • Real airflow over the fan, hidden in the line.

Watch

  • Needs one spool of translucent filament.
  • A visible frosted line when off, and dust can settle in the gaps.
  • The insert needs a precise fit; one test print to tune it.
Concept C · 52 LEDs · 314 mm path

Floating lid

The top 6 mm becomes a separate lid that seems to float on a 3 mm band of light running round all four sides. The band is a frosted ring set slightly back. Air moves through the seam above and below it.

Diffuser

A frosted ring in translucent white filament, set into the seam.

Air

Through the seam around the lid: the largest, most even vent of the four.

Parts

Body, lid, frosted ring, base, light channel. One more than now.

Good

  • Seen from eye level and from every side, not just from above.
  • The most distinctive silhouette; the lid really reads as floating.
  • Best airflow.

Watch

  • The top surface stays plain, so the light doesn't trace the top face from above.
  • Needs translucent filament, and the lid needs hidden posts to hold it.
Concept D · Wildcard · 52 LEDs · 326 mm path

Underglow

The shell sits on a small hidden base, 5 mm off the table. The light shines down and spreads onto whatever it sits on. Not a top ring, but worth seeing once.

Diffuser

The table. No translucent filament.

Air

Underneath, through the gap around the base.

Parts

Shell, smaller base, light channel.

Good

  • Calm and ambient; the device appears to hover.
  • Nothing to diffuse, so no new filament.

Watch

  • The look depends on the surface: bright on a white shelf, faint on dark wood.
  • Hard to read a status at a glance.

LEDs, power and wiring

LEDs

A WS2812B addressable strip at 144 LEDs per metre: 48–52 LEDs, about 7 mm apart. Every LED can be any colour, so light can flow, chase, breathe or show gradients like these.

Mounting

Strips bend through their thickness but not sideways, so the strip stands on edge around the inside of a printed black channel that follows the path. The channel also stops light leaking into the rest of the shell.

Connected to the Pi only

Three wires: 5 V (pin 2), ground (pin 6), data (pin 19, the Pi's SPI output). A diode, resistor and capacitor in line, as in the rev C plan. Software caps brightness so the strip draws under about 0.8 A.

Recommendation

Which I'd build

C · Floating lid, with D's underglow as an optional extra

C is the only top-perimeter concept that still reads in a lit room, and it's visible from every side, not just from above. When off it becomes a crisp shadow seam that looks deliberate, and the seam gives the fan the most airflow of any option. It follows the shape of the top face exactly, just on the sides rather than on the surface.

If you specifically want the light on the top surface, B is the better of the two top-face options: it stays vivid at night. Expect it to look faint by day. A is the most elegant when off, but it only works in a dim room and leaves a faint outline when off. D was the surprise: the most legible in daylight and a lovely night light. It's easy to add under C later, since both use the same kind of strip.
