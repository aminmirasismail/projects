Dry brushes head-down

Four ways to hang Chelsea's eight brushes upside down while they dry, each with a different way to fold away. All hold the brushes by the handle in the same snap clip, all are white PLA with black for the base and small accents, and all fit the A1 Mini's bed.

Brush sizes are placeholders until you measure them: four thick brushes with 11 mm handles and four thin ones with 7 mm handles, 172 and 146 mm long. Click any image to zoom; click again for full size.

Research

Why upside down

Keep water out of the ferrule

The metal collar holds the bristles in with glue. Dried upright, rinse water runs down into it, softens the glue and the brush starts shedding. Hanging head-down lets gravity pull water out through the tips instead.

Let the head hang free

Lying flat squashes one side and dries slowly; a cup traps water. The usual advice is to squeeze, reshape, and hang the brush with nothing touching the bristles and air all round. So every concept spaces the heads apart and keeps them clear of the floor.

No heat, catch the drips

Hair dryers and radiators soften the glue too, so air drying overnight is the norm. Wet brushes drip for the first while, so A, B and D have a black drip tray under the heads. C drips onto the counter or a towel.

Concept A · Telescope · 9 parts

A post that slides down

A round black drip dish with a three-stage white post. The clip disc sits on top with the eight brushes round its rim, thick and thin alternating. Pull the disc up and the post telescopes out; black twist collars lock each stage. Pushed down, it stands about 10 cm tall.

Size

Ø164 mm dish. Disc top 165 mm high open; about 103 mm stored.

Parts

Dish, 3 post tubes, 2 lock collars, disc, knob. Black: dish, collars, knob.

Printing

Tubes print upright, disc and dish flat. All fit the bed, no supports.

Good

  • Evenly spaced brushes, easy to reach from any side.
  • Drips land in the dish.
  • Looks the most like a finished product.

Watch

  • Sliding joints rock unless they overlap a lot. I've set 30 mm overlaps; they also need real bayonet locks, which I haven't designed yet.
  • Only shrinks to about 60% of its height.
  • Most work to get right in PLA.
Concept B · Folio · 6 parts

Folds flat like a closed book

A black drip tray with a white back panel hinged along its rear edge. A clip shelf hangs off the top of the panel. Thick brushes snap in from the front; thin ones snap in from behind, through a 32 mm gap between the shelf and the panel. To store, the shelf swings down against the panel and the panel folds forward onto the tray.

Size

176 × 160 mm. Shelf 165 mm high open; about 26 mm thick folded.

Parts

Tray, panel, shelf, hinge pins. Black: tray and pins.

Printing

All parts print flat. The 176 mm tray leaves no room on the bed for a brim.

Good

  • Flattest when stored: slides into a drawer.
  • Wide, low tray that is hard to tip.
  • Flat parts: quick, clean prints.

Watch

  • Nothing holds it open yet. Both hinges need hard 90° stops plus a latch, or the brushes' weight folds the panel forward.
  • The back row is awkward to load.
  • Takes the most counter space when open.
Concept C · Fan · 11 parts

Arms that swing into one line

A black drip dish with a white post. Four white arms stack on the post with black spacers and swivel independently, with a clip at each end. Fanned out at 45° they hold eight brushes in a ring; swung together, they stack into one narrow bar.

Size

Ø164 mm dish, arms 128 mm long, 176 mm tall. Same height stored.

Parts

Dish, post, 4 arms, 4 spacers, cap. Black: dish, spacers, cap.

Printing

Arms and dish flat; post upright.

Good

  • Satisfying to use.
  • Arms can be set to any angle to space big heads further apart.

Watch

  • Doesn't really collapse: it only gets narrower, and the dish stays round.
  • Swivel arms have play: about 4 mm at the tips.
  • Tallest, so the easiest to tip when you push a brush in.
Concept D · Flat-pack · 4 parts

No moving parts, stacks flat

Two white plates slot together into a cross, standing in a cross-shaped socket in the black drip dish. The clip disc drops onto the plates' tops. The brushes hang in the four bays between the plates, two per bay, so wet heads never swing into each other. To store, lift it apart and stack the three white parts on the dish.

Size

Ø164 mm dish, disc top 165 mm high. About 35 mm tall packed.

Parts

Dish, two plates, disc. Black: dish only.

Printing

All flat, no supports. Plates 128 × 156 mm.

Good

  • Sturdiest: the cross joints engage about 80 mm, so it's stiff every way, with no hinge play.
  • Simplest to print and to get right first time.
  • The plates keep wet heads apart.

Watch

  • Packs down by taking apart, not folding.
  • The disc only rests on the plates; it needs snap tabs so pulling a brush out doesn't lift it.
  • The plates' corners overhang the dish when packed.
Shared by all four

The snap clip

What the renders show: a keyhole in an 8 mm plate. You push the handle in sideways through an opening slightly narrower than it. Two curved relief slots turn the sides into springy jaws that open about 0.8 mm and close round the handle, and friction holds the brush up.

The review says this clip won't last in PLA

Opening the jaws 0.5–0.8 mm strains the PLA close to its yield point (about 1.5%), and pushing a brush in takes tens of newtons sideways. That is enough to tip a stand this tall. PLA held open also creeps, so the grip fades over weeks, and repeated use will crack the jaws within months. The geometry stays as a placeholder until we choose one of the two fixes below.

Fix 1 (recommended): soft grommet, loaded from below

Use a plain hole in the white PLA plate with a black rubber grommet in it: about 1.5 mm thick, 6–8 radial slits, a hole about 70% of the handle diameter. You push the handle's tail up through it from underneath. The force is vertical, so nothing tips, and one grommet fits a range of handle sizes. It needs either black TPU filament (a new spool) or off-the-shelf silicone grommets.

Fix 2: PLA only, a longer and gentler spring

Use jaws at least 18 mm long and 1.2 mm thick (three lines), with rounded slot ends. Strain falls to about 0.35% and the push force to about 1.5 N per mm. The clip then works as a light keeper sized to each handle, not a tight grip. That's fine for 10–40 g brushes, but each clip must be sized to one handle.

Recommendation

D · Flat-pack, with the grommet grip

It's the sturdiest by a margin: nothing slides or hinges, so nothing wobbles or wears loose. It is also the simplest and most reliable to print. It packs to a 35 mm stack. If Chelsea wants it to fold into a drawer-thin slab instead, B is the pick, but its hinges need more design work before it is as solid. Whichever you choose, I'd switch to grommets loaded from below, and add snap tabs or rubber feet as needed.

What I need from you

Pick a concept and a grip

Choose a concept, or mix them, for example D's cross on B's rectangular tray. Then choose the grip: grommets (TPU spool or bought silicone) or the PLA-only spring clip.

Measure each brush

  • Handle diameter about 40 mm from the handle end (where it's gripped).
  • Overall length.
  • Widest point of the head when fluffed.

Calipers are best; a ruler across the flats is fine for a start.

Then I'll

Size each clip to its brush, set the height so the longest brush clears the tray, print a small clip test strip for the snap fit, and send you the full set of renders, drawings and the sliced plate for approval.
