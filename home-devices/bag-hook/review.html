Tote Bag Hook

A small white plate with two 45° pegs for your four empty Costco and Trader Joe's totes, two per peg. It has no screws and no adhesive. A thin tongue on the back drops into the tab of each 3M CLAW drywall hanger, the way picture wire would.

Before you print

Two things to know

Drywall only, not the door. 3M says the CLAW is for drywall only. Its steel claw needs the gypsum core to bite into. On a solid wood door it won't go in, and on a hollow-core door the skin is too thin to hold it. Mount this on the wall beside the door. If you want it on the door itself, I can make an over-the-door version instead.

Fit test 1 passed (rev B): it hung well and took 3–5 mm of lift to release. The tongue's bottom edge sagged a little. Rev C replaces the sag with a breakaway fin and thins the tip so it seats deeper in the V, aiming for about 5 mm of lift. A second fit test confirms the number.

The CLAW's tab is estimated, so test before the long print. I couldn't measure a real CLAW. I read the tab from 3M's drawings: about 7 mm wide and 9 mm tall, leaning out about 28°. If the tab leans less than about 20°, the tongue barely catches it. The fit test is a 30 mm slice through one bay, printed at the same settings as the hook. Push one CLAW into the wall, hang the slice on it, and check:

  1. Snap out the fin first. In each bay, a thin fin stands under the tongue's tip. Push it sideways with a small screwdriver or pliers and it breaks off at its base.
  2. It drops on without forcing and its back sits flat on the wall.
  3. Its top edge is about 8 mm above the CLAW's top. If it's clearly higher, the tab is shallower than I assumed, and the tongue is catching less of it.
  4. You have to lift it about 5 mm before it comes off (4.7 mm estimated). A pull straight out at the bottom shouldn't release it.
  5. Even better, if you can: measure one CLAW's tab (its height, and how far its top edge stands off the plate). Then I can set the tongue to the real part.

How it hangs

A tongue in the CLAW's V

The CLAW is a 23 mm square steel plate. A tab punched from its middle is hinged at the bottom and leans out, making a V that opens upward. Picture wire and D-rings drop into it. A tongue runs along the back of the hook, 0.34 mm in front of the plate, and drops into the same V. Its front face tapers to a 0.5 mm edge at the tip, so it seats about 3.2 mm up the V, and it thickens to 2.2 mm at the root. Lifting it off takes about 5 mm (estimated from a 28° tab; fit test 1 measured 3–5 mm with the old tip). The bags' weight wedges it down. The tab stops the top pulling off the wall, and the bottom 29 mm of the plate rests flat against the wall below the CLAWs.

  1. Mark two dots 80 mm apart and level, 31 mm below where you want the hook's top edge. Use a pencil or the CLAW spot markers.
  2. Push in the CLAWs as 3M describes: put the notch in each CLAW's bottom edge on a dot, then push the top in until it sits flush. Each CLAW's top ends up about 8 mm below the hook's top edge.
  3. Snap out the five fins under the tongue in the back bays (see the fit-test note).
  4. Hang the hook: hold it flat on the wall about 5 mm above its final spot, then slide it down until the tongue drops into both tabs. The tabs go in bays 1 and 5 on the back. Anything from 60 to 90 mm apart still lands in them. Keep the CLAWs level: 1 mm of difference tilts the hook visibly.
  5. To take it off, lift it about 5 mm and pull it away.
  6. Optional anti-lift: nothing but gravity holds the hook down. If lifting off the last tote ever unhooks it, put a small removable adhesive strip on the flat back below the recess.

Design review

What the independent review found

Rev B
Your changes: longer pegs, square bottom corners.The pegs go from 48 to 60 mm. The hook now sticks out 60 mm instead of 52, and the tips are 4 mm below its top edge. The longer pegs put its centre of mass over the front edge of its footprint while it prints, so it now prints turned 90° on the plate. Its 120 mm length then runs with the bed's front-to-back motion. The bottom corners are square. The CLAW interface and the fit test haven't changed.
Fixed
Each peg root had a chin printing in mid-air.The peg started only 4 mm inside the plate, so its 45° start cap poked out below the root. It's now embedded 8 mm, and the root starts cleanly on the face.
Fixed
The CLAW spacing advice was wrong.At 40 mm apart, a peg sits outside the support span, and one tote on it tips the hook. Use bays 1 and 5 (80 mm, anything from 60 to 90 works).
Fixed
The tongue root was weak in bending.The front face now drafts from 1.2 mm at the tip to 2.2 mm at the root, about 3× less stress. Plate clearance went from 0.24 to 0.34 mm, to allow for paint.
Fixed
The peg roots sat partly over the bays.I raised the bay floor from 24 to 31 mm, so both roots are on solid plastic. The print now uses 4 walls and 20% infill.
Fixed
The fit test wasn't printed like the hook.It now uses the same 0.08 mm settings, so the tongue's bridged edge matches. The page also lists what it has to pass.
Fixed
No brim on a 70 mm tall, 12 mm thin part.It now has a 4 mm brim on the hidden underside.
Fixed
The top corners didn't need the 45° flats.They're full R10 arcs now. In rev B, at your request, the bottom corners are square instead of rounded.
Fixed
Errors in the drawings.Peg outlines were idealised, the label size was the font size, and the section lacked heights and "est." marks. All corrected. The in-use render now shows 26 mm handles.
Open
Gravity is the only thing holding it down.A sharp upward pull, like yanking the last tote off, could lift the tongue out. I raised the peg end round to R3 so handles don't snag. If it ever happens, the adhesive-strip tip above fixes it.
Rev C
The tongue's lower edge sagged on fit test 1.You suggested a tree support there. Bambu's tree support filled every bay up to its roof and grew in from behind, adding 40 minutes. Instead, each bay now has a 0.6 mm breakaway fin under the tip, stopping two layers (0.16 mm) short of it. You snap the fins out after printing. Checked in the toolpaths: the tip prints onto the fin instead of sagging into the air.
Rev C
About 5 mm of lift to release, as you asked.Making the tongue longer wouldn't help: how far it seats in the V decides the lift. The tip now tapers to a 0.5 mm edge, so it seats about 1.3 mm deeper. Estimated lift is 4.7 mm, up from 3.5. The hook sits about 1.3 mm lower on the CLAWs.
Kept
Printing on its side for stronger pegs.The layers would run along the pegs, but the pegs would need supports or a diamond cross-section. It would also stand 120 mm tall on a 14 mm edge. Upright, the peg root has 30× margin for empty totes and over 3× for 5 kg on one tip.
Kept
No fillet at the peg root.Stress there is low for empty totes, and the plain dowel look is deliberate.
